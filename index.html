To get the very top marks

  1. Answer every question. Never leave one blank: if you are not sure, take your best guess.
  2. Read every question carefully and think about which answer you choose.
  3. Know the definitions of all the key words. Practise them in the Flashcards.
  4. Don’t just memorise the stories. Know the different meanings and interpretations of them.
  5. For written answers, use PEEL. Point: answer the question. Evidence: a belief, teaching, thinker or example. Explain: why it matters. Link back to the question. For “evaluate” questions, add a paragraph that starts However, and finish with a judgement.

Part 1

What is a worldview?

Philosophy · How do humans find meaning?

Key ideas

  • A worldview is the way a person understands the world.
  • Everyone has a worldview: nobody stands nowhere.
  • Personal worldviews may or may not be religious, and may or may not be part of an organised worldview.
  • Both organised and personal worldviews can change over time.

Subjective or objective?

  • Subjective: based on a person’s own feelings, views or experiences.
  • Objective: based on facts that are the same for everyone.
  • Your worldview makes you see things in a subjective way.
Remember: Organised worldview = shared by a group. Personal worldview = the way YOU see the world.

How are worldviews shaped?

Philosophy · How do humans find meaning?

Five influences

  • Religion: the beliefs and practices a person follows or grows up with.
  • Time or era: when you live. People 500 years ago saw the world differently.
  • Culture and norms: the customs and normal ways of life around you.
  • Geography and society: where you live and the people around you.
  • Life experiences: things that happen to you, good and bad.

Thinking about yourself

  • Positionality: how your experiences, beliefs and background shape the way you see the world.
  • Reflective: taking time to think about what happens to us and how our actions affect others.
  • Reflexivity: understanding how your own thoughts and actions affect you and the people around you.
Remember: Learn two ways a worldview can be shaped, with an example for each.

Part 3

What is in the Bible?

Theology · How do humans find meaning?

Key facts

  • The Bible is a collection of books, like a library.
  • It was written by about 40 authors over about 1,500 years.
  • Most Protestant Bibles have 66 books. Catholic Bibles have 73.
  • The Old Testament is about events before the life of Jesus.
  • The New Testament is about the life of Jesus and what happened afterwards.

Authors and worldviews

  • The authors wrote at different times, in different languages, and had different jobs.
  • Each author’s worldview influenced how they wrote.
  • Your own worldview also influences how YOU understand what you read.
  • So it is important to listen to other people’s interpretations too.
Remember: About 40 authors, about 1,500 years, two main parts: Old Testament and New Testament.

Part 4

How is Genesis understood?

Theology · How do humans find meaning?

What is Genesis?

  • Genesis is the first book of the Bible. The word means beginning.
  • It tells the story of creation.
  • It was likely written by Moses, but some Bible scholars disagree.

Lost in translation

  • The Bible was not first written in English, so words can be translated in different ways.
  • The Hebrew word yom is translated as “day”.
  • But yom can mean a 24-hour day, or a much longer period of time.
  • So “six days” might mean six long ages.
Remember: Literal = word for word, true as written. Non-literal = it is the message that matters.

Part 5

Dominion, stewardship and Humanism

Philosophy and Social Sciences · How do humans find meaning?

How do Humanists find meaning?

  • Humanists do not believe in God or a god-given purpose.
  • They believe there is no set purpose to life, so people can create their own meaning.
  • They find meaning by seeking happiness and helping others to be happy too.
  • Many Humanists care for the environment because this is the only life and the only planet we have.

Religious and non-religious care

  • Christians may care for the Earth because of stewardship.
  • Humanists may care for the Earth because of reason and concern for future people.
  • Different worldviews can lead to the same action, for different reasons.
Remember: Dominion = control. Stewardship = responsibility to look after. Humanists create their own meaning.

Part 6

What do we mean by ‘religion’?

Social Sciences · What is a religion?

Key ideas

  • There is no single definition of religion that everyone agrees on.
  • The word has different meanings and translations in different languages.
  • Religions share several features, such as beliefs, practices, communities and holy places.

Why does it matter?

  • Using only the English word can make us see every religion like Christianity.
  • Many Hindus prefer the word dharma, and many Muslims use deen.
  • Thinking about your own positionality helps you avoid this.
Remember: Religion, dharma and deen: three words, three different meanings.

Part 7

Ninian Smart’s seven dimensions

Social Sciences · What is a religion?

Who was Ninian Smart?

  • A British scholar who studied world religions.
  • He did not try to give one definition. Instead he described seven dimensions (features) that religions have.
  • A dimension is one part or aspect of something.

Using the dimensions

  • Match an example to a dimension. A wedding ceremony = ritual.
  • The Ten Commandments = ethical. A gurdwara or a mosque = physical.
  • Worshipping together = social. Feeling peace when praying = experience.
Remember: Seven dimensions: Ritual, Stories, Experience, Social, Ethical, Teachings, Physical.

Part 8

How do social scientists understand religion?

Social Sciences · What is a religion?

Three ways to understand religion

  • What it contains: some say a religion must have certain things, like belief in God, prophets or a holy book.
  • What it does: some focus on its purpose: bringing people together and giving them a shared purpose.
  • Who made it: some think religion was created by people in power, so it is a social construct.

Key words

  • Social construct: an idea created and accepted by the people in a society.
  • Constructionist: someone who thinks religion is a social construct, so it is different for different people.
  • Émile Durkheim: a French sociologist who studied what religion does for society.
Remember: Durkheim: religion brings people together, so they share the same beliefs and values. Rituals help with this.
